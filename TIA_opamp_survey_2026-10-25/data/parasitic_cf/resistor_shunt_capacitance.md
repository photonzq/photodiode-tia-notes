# Shunt capacitance of chip resistors, from primary sources

This file collects published values of the capacitance that appears in parallel with a surface-mount chip resistor (from one terminal to the other), by package size and film type. It is meant to ground the resistor-body part of the total feedback capacitance assumed for a 2-kΩ feedback resistor in a ≥150-MHz photodiode transimpedance amplifier. Pad and layout capacitance are covered elsewhere and appear here only where a source cannot separate them from the body. The machine-readable version, with every field of every record, is `resistor_shunt_capacitance.json` in this folder; the point-by-point curve readings behind the derived values are in `resistor_curve_readings.csv`.

## Why the number matters at 2 kΩ

A capacitance $C$ across a resistor $R$ puts a pole at $f_c = 1/(2\pi R C)$ in the resistor's own impedance. For $R = 2\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ this gives $f_c = 2.65\ \mathrm{GHz}$ for $C = 30\ \mathrm{fF}$, $1.59\ \mathrm{GHz}$ for $50\ \mathrm{fF}$, $0.66\ \mathrm{GHz}$ for $120\ \mathrm{fF}$ and $0.40\ \mathrm{GHz}$ for $200\ \mathrm{fF}$, so the body capacitance is a large part of the parasitic feedback capacitance, which Table 1.1 of the note takes as 0.08 pF, with corners at 0.05 and 0.12 pF, from the estimate of its Appendix G.

The series inductance $L_s$ of the chip barely matters at this resistance. For the three-element model (R in series with $L_s$, both shunted by $C_p$) the effective parallel capacitance at low frequency is $C_\mathrm{eff} \approx C_p - L_s/R^2$; with the largest inductance found here for a 0603 part, $L_s = 1.1\ \mathrm{nH}$, and $R = 2\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, the correction is $1.1\times10^{-9}/(2000)^2 = 2.8\times10^{-16}\ \mathrm{F} = 0.28\ \mathrm{fF}$, which is negligible.

## How the values were obtained

Every number in the table was either read by eye on a rendered page (PDF pages rendered to PNG at 120–220 dpi, or the downloaded web images) or computed here from a plotted curve. The computed values are marked "derived". For those, the curve was read numerically: from the vector paths of the PDF when the plot is vector graphics (Vishay FC, CH and Beyschlag documents), or from the bitmap when it is not (Vishay TN 60107, Fig. 4). Each point of $|Z|/R$ at frequency $f$ was converted into the capacitance that a pure parallel R–C would need to produce it,

$$C_\mathrm{eq}(f) = \frac{\sqrt{(R/|Z|)^2 - 1}}{2\pi f R},$$

and the median and spread over the points with $0.15 < |Z|/R < 0.95$ are reported. A flat $C_\mathrm{eq}(f)$ shows that the curve behaves like a single parallel capacitance in that band. The scripts are in `scripts_archive/parasitic_cf/` (`dump_pdf_vector_paths.py`, `read_vector_impedance_curves.py`, `trace_raster_curve.py`, `merge_curve_readings.py`, with the axis calibrations in `curve_specs/`).

## The sources one by one

**Vishay technical note 60107, "Frequency Response of Thin Film Chip Resistors" (Rev. 04-Feb-09).** This is the most complete vendor study. Modelithics measured S-parameters from 0.1 to 40 GHz on NiCr thin-film parts of 50–1000 Ω in 0201, 0402 and 0603, mounted on quartz microstrip boards with the RF ground plane on the back, and fitted a lumped model in which an internal shunt capacitance $C$ bridges $R$ in series with $L$, while external connection inductances $L_c$ and capacitances to ground $C_g$ sit at each end and the landing pads are modelled as transmission lines (Fig. 3, p. 2). Table 1 on p. 1 gives the internal $C$ as 20.6 fF (0201), 26.2 fF (0402 flip chip), 39.2 fF (0402 wrap-around) and 40.3 fF (0603 flip chip). The note states on p. 3 that the internal coefficients do not depend strongly on size or part value and that Table 1 gives averages; Fig. 7 on p. 3 shows the external $C_g$ to be roughly constant with part value at about 8, 27 and 52 fF at 1 kΩ for 0201, 0402 and 0603 (read by eye). The measured and modelled $|Z_\mathrm{in}|/R$ curves of Figs. 4–6 roll off faster than the internal $C$ alone would make them; for the 0201 at 1 kΩ (Fig. 4) the curve corresponds to $C_\mathrm{eq} \approx 32\ \mathrm{fF}$ (model line) to 36 fF (measured symbols), against 20.6 fF internal. The note does not say whether $|Z_\mathrm{in}|$ was formed from a one-port or a two-port measurement, so the extra capacitance cannot be assigned with certainty, but it is of the size of the end capacitance $C_g$ plus pad sections.

**Vishay FC datasheet 60093 (Rev. 11-May-2026), p. 3.** The current datasheet repeats the same $C$ and $L$ values beside its internal-impedance plots and states that the model applies at the bonding pad–resistor reference plane. Reading the plotted curves numerically gives a flat $C_\mathrm{eq}$ of about 68 fF (0402 flip chip, 1 kΩ), 70 fF (0402 wrap-around, 1 kΩ) and 74 fF (0603 flip chip, 1 kΩ) over roughly 1–14 GHz, which is 1.8 to 2.6 times the printed internal $C$. So the same document carries two "capacitances", and which one applies across a TIA feedback resistor depends on where the field lines end in the real layout.

**Vishay Sfernice CH datasheet 53014 (Rev. 03-Jul-2026), pp. 6–8.** The CH series (10–500 Ω, rated to 70 GHz) shows "internal impedance" curves computed from R, L and C only, and states on p. 6 that R, L and C belong to the chip itself while $L_c$ and $C_g$ depend on the mounting. The values of $C$ are not printed, so they were recovered from the curves: 20 fF (02016, flip and one-face), 25 fF (0402 flip and one-face), 40 fF (0402 wrap-around), 40 fF (0603 flip and one-face) and 49 fF (0603 wrap-around). The 500-Ω and 250-Ω curves give the same $C$ within a few per cent, and the slightly lower values at 150 Ω agree with the inductance term $L/R^2$. These recovered values match TN 60107 for the three cases both documents cover, which also confirms the curve-reading method.

**Vishay Beyschlag application note 28871, "Resistors in Microwave Applications" (Rev. 19-Mar-13).** Standard precision thin-film flat chips with meander trim (MCT 0603, MCU 0805) and MELF parts were measured with an HP 8720B network analyser as series elements between 50-Ω ports from 0.1 to 20 GHz. The table on p. 4 lists $C = 35\ \mathrm{fF}$ for the MCT 0603 at 6.8, 50 and 470 Ω with $L$ = 0.58, 1.0 and 1.53 nH; the identical 35 fF for every value and for the MELF suggests that $C$ may have been held fixed while $L$ was fitted (p. 3 speaks of "the required L for a given C"). The plotted 470-Ω curve gives $C_\mathrm{eq}$ = 31–36 fF between 3 and 11 GHz, consistent with the table, and the MCU 0805 470-Ω curve (Fig. 7, p. 5) gives 35–37 fF between 7 and 12 GHz. The fixture and its de-embedding are not described.

**KOA technical note KDST_TN-010_v0100-EN, "High-frequency characteristics of resistors" (2021).** KOA gives three-element parameters for its RK73 thick-film flat chips, calculated from impedance-analyser measurements (Table 1, p. 3): $C_p$ = 12 fF (01005), 23 fF (0201), 30 fF (0402), 50 fF (0603) and 65 fF (0805), with $L_s$ from 0.37 to 1.1 nH. The note states that these parameters are virtually independent of resistance value because the structure changes little, and calls them typical values. Table 2 shows that the wide-terminal WK73R parts have two to three times larger $C_p$ for the same footprint (82 fF for 0204, 95 fF for 0306), which illustrates that the electrode geometry, not the film, sets the capacitance. The fixture, compensation and frequency range are not stated. KOA also offers per-series equivalent-circuit PDFs (including the RN73 thin-film series) on its design-support page, but the download requires agreeing to a disclaimer, so they were not fetched.

**Schurr, Judaschke and Awan, Meas. Sci. Technol. 36, 015030 (2025), preprint arXiv:2407.17805.** This metrology paper from PTB and the University of Plymouth is the only source with stated uncertainties. Single thin-film resistors were measured free-standing in an SMD test fixture of an impedance analyser between 10 and 110 MHz, where the parallel capacitance was found practically independent of frequency, and the result was checked against a CST simulation of the resistor floating in free space. Table 1 (p. 7) gives $C_P = 83 \pm 7\ \mathrm{fF}$ for Susumu RG 0805 parts of 3.0 and 3.3 kΩ (simulated $115 \pm 16$ fF) and $123 \pm 7\ \mathrm{fF}$ for Vishay TNPU/TNPW 0603 parts of 1.0 and 1.3 kΩ (simulated $125 \pm 7$ fF), with $k = 1$. The values were also confirmed on the publisher's page of the journal version. These are the only measured numbers at kilo-ohm values in 0603 and 0805, but they describe a part with no ground plane and no board dielectric nearby.

**Adamczyk, Koeller and Healy, "Estimating the Parasitics of Passive Circuit Components", In Compliance Magazine, April 2022.** Three resistors on a small SMA test board were measured as one-port loads on a vector network analyser, with open, short and load standards on the same board at the same trace length, and $C$ was taken from the −3-dB frequency as $C = 1/(2\pi f R)$ (Equation 2, Table 1). The 3-kΩ part gives 64 fF from a −3-dB point at 833.5 MHz, which is a plausible figure for a part on a board. The 300-Ω result (276 fF at 1.92 GHz) is distorted by series inductance and the 31.6-kΩ result (225 fF at 22.4 MHz) is from a trace with resonant structure far from 50 Ω, and the authors say the approach does not target accuracy. The package and the resistor make are not stated.

**Andersson and Fahd, "High frequency modeling of SMD resistors", MSc thesis, Chalmers University of Technology (2024).** This is a simulation study of generic 50–110-Ω resistors for millimetre-wave use. For a 0402 50-Ω model on a thin microstrip board it extracts, at 42 GHz, $C_p$ = 8.7 fF between the terminals and $C_g$ = 39 fF to ground (PDF p. 51). Its substrate sweep (Fig. 4.18, PDF p. 63) shows $C_p$ rising only from 9.2 to 10.0 fF while $C_g$ falls from 34.5 to 24.7 fF as the board thickness goes from 4.7 to 13 mil. The values come from a generic geometry and from an extraction at 42 GHz, so they say little about a real kilo-ohm part, and they are listed for completeness.

## What this means for a 2-kΩ resistor in 0201, 0402 and 0603

Over a ground plane, the reported body capacitance is consistent across vendors at about 20–25 fF for 0201, 25–40 fF for 0402 and 35–50 fF for 0603, although none of these vendor values carries an uncertainty. Vishay's thin-film fits, the curves of its CH datasheet, Vishay Beyschlag's standard 0603 and KOA's thick-film table all fall in these bands, and thin and thick film differ less than flip-chip and wrap-around terminations do (26 versus 39 fF in 0402). The only uncertainty-quantified measurement, however, gives $123 \pm 7$ fF for a free-standing 0603 thin-film part of 1.0–1.3 kΩ and $83 \pm 7$ fF for a 0805 of 3.0–3.3 kΩ, which is two to three times the over-ground values. A comparable free-standing measurement for 0402 or 0201 does not exist. The leading explanation is that a nearby ground plane or fixture electrode turns part of the end-to-end capacitance into capacitance to ground. This is an inference, because no source measures the same part both ways and the one controlled simulation (Chalmers) shows only a weak effect of board thickness. The sources therefore disagree mainly on bookkeeping, that is, on what counts as $C$ across the resistor and what counts as $C_g$, and this depends on the board, the ground plane and the fixture compensation.

None of the sources measures exactly 2 kΩ on an FR-4 TIA layout. The statements that $C$ is nearly independent of $R$ (KOA for thick film, Vishay for thin film up to 1 kΩ) support carrying the 50–1000-Ω data over to 2 kΩ, since $L_s/R^2$ is below 0.3 fF there. No source in this set covers high-value thin film (10 kΩ and above, with finer meanders), so the claim of independence from $R$ is untested in that range. The resistor body therefore accounts for roughly 0.02–0.05 pF in 0201–0603 when the part sits over a ground plane, and up to about 0.12 pF for a 0603 with nothing beneath it. The difference between the curve-derived $C_\mathrm{eq}$ of 68–74 fF for Vishay's 0402/0603 parts and their stated internal $C$ of 26–40 fF shows the same thing from a single dataset: how much of the termination capacitance bridges the resistor depends on the layout.

## Sources checked but without usable numbers

The Vishay CHEP datasheet (doc. 53090) shows the model topology only, the Vishay Beyschlag "Basics of Linear Fixed Resistors" note (doc. 28771) has a qualitative paragraph, and the Vishay Sfernice CHA application note (doc. 53087, 2024) describes measurement to 70 GHz of 50–100-Ω parts without giving $C$. The Vishay D/CRCW thick-film and MC precision thin-film datasheets contain no high-frequency data. These files were not kept.

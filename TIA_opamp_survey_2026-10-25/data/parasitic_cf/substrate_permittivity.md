# Permittivity of low-loss board materials that could replace FR-4

Part of the capacitance across a chip resistor comes from the field that runs through the board under and between its pads, so a board material with a lower relative permittivity under the resistor lowers that part. This file collects the relative permittivity (Dk, the real part of the relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r$) of seven low-loss substrates from the manufacturers' current data sheets, together with the dissipation factor (Df, $\tan\delta$) wherever the same table gives it, and states on which page and in which table or chart each value is printed. The band that matters for the capacitance estimate is 0.1 to 1 GHz, but most of these data sheets quote Dk only at 10 GHz, so the paragraph for each material says what, if anything, its document gives at or below 1 GHz. Page indices count from 0, as a PDF library counts them; printed page numbers are quoted as printed, and the DuPont and Isola sheets print none. The machine-readable version, with one record per document page, is `substrate_permittivity.json` in this folder, and the documents are in `appnotes/parasitic_cf/materials/substrates/`.

Rogers gives two kinds of Dk; for RO4350B and RO4003C they differ by more than the tolerance, while for RO3003 and RT/duroid 5880 they are equal. The process Dk is measured with IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.5, a clamped-stripline test at 10 GHz, and serves for quality acceptance; Rogers prints it with a tolerance. The design Dk comes from a microstrip differential-phase-length measurement over 8 to 40 GHz and is an average over several lots and the most common thicknesses; it is the value Rogers gives for circuit design, and the RT/duroid data sheet warns that the process values may not suit design work, particularly for microstrip. Panasonic, DuPont and Isola print single typical values without a tolerance. Numbers read from a chart rather than from a table are marked "about" and come with their reading resolution.

## What each data sheet states

### Rogers RO4350B

The RO4000 data sheet (Rogers PUB# 92-004, revision code 1592 080322, © 2022, four pages) covers RO4003C and RO4350B together. Its property table on page index 2 (printed "Page 3 of 4") gives RO4350B, in the z direction, a process Dk of $3.48 \pm 0.05$ at 10 GHz and 23 °C by IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.5 clamped stripline and a design Dk of $3.66$ for 8 to 40 GHz by the differential phase length method; the dissipation factor in the same table is $0.0037$ at 10 GHz and $0.0031$ at 2.5 GHz (IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.5, 23 °C). Note (1) under the table gives 4 mil RO4350B laminates a process Dk of $3.33 \pm 0.05$, and note (2) describes the design Dk as an average over several lots measured on the most common thicknesses. The paragraph below the notes gives the Dk of the LoPro resin as about $2.4$, says that the tabulated design Dk should still be used for LoPro laminates, and says that the design Dk falls by about $0.1$ as the core thickness goes from 0.020 in to 0.004 in. The thermal coefficient of Dk is $+50$ ppm/°C from $-50$ °C to $150$ °C, and the moisture absorption is 0.06 % (48 h immersion of a 0.060 in sample at 50 °C, ASTM D570). Chart 2 on page index 1 (printed "Page 2 of 4") plots Dk against frequency on a logarithmic axis from 1 GHz to about 50 GHz, measured with the microstrip differential phase length method. Read by eye against its gridlines, which are spaced 0.01 apart, to about $\pm 0.005$, the 20 mil RO4350B curve is about $3.76$ at 1 GHz, $3.75$ at 2 GHz, $3.73$ at 10 GHz and $3.70$ near 50 GHz, and the 20.7 mil RO4350B LoPro curve is about $3.665$ at 1 GHz and $3.64$ at 10 GHz. The chart starts at 1 GHz, so the document gives nothing below 1 GHz. Its three numbers at 10 GHz do not agree: the clamped-stripline process Dk ($3.48$) is the lowest, the tabulated design Dk ($3.66$) lies close to the LoPro curve, and the 20 mil microstrip curve ($3.73$) is the highest. The data sheet does not reconcile them beyond describing the design Dk as an average that depends on core thickness.

### Rogers RO4003C

The same property table (page index 2, printed "Page 3 of 4") gives RO4003C a process Dk of $3.38 \pm 0.05$ at 10 GHz and 23 °C by IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.5 clamped stripline and a design Dk of $3.55$ for 8 to 40 GHz by the differential phase length method, with a dissipation factor of $0.0027$ at 10 GHz and $0.0021$ at 2.5 GHz. The thermal coefficient of Dk is $+40$ ppm/°C from $-50$ °C to $150$ °C, and the moisture absorption is 0.06 %. The first page (index 0, printed "Page 1 of 4") states that RO4003C is offered with 1080 and 1674 glass styles and that all of them meet the same electrical specification. On Chart 2 (page index 1, printed "Page 2 of 4") the 20 mil RO4003C curve is about $3.69$ at 1 GHz, $3.68$ at 2 GHz, $3.65$ at 10 GHz and $3.62$ near 50 GHz, and the 20.7 mil RO4003C LoPro curve is about $3.56$ at 1 GHz and $3.52$ to $3.53$ at 10 GHz, read to about $\pm 0.005$. As for RO4350B, the chart starts at 1 GHz, and at 10 GHz the 20 mil curve lies above both the process Dk and the design Dk, while the LoPro curve lies close to the design Dk.

### Rogers RO3003

The RO3000 data sheet (Rogers Publication #92-130, revision code 1687 013024, © 2024, four pages) lists, in the Standard Properties Table on page index 1 (printed "2"), a process Dk for RO3003 of $3.00 \pm 0.04$ at 10 GHz and 23 °C by IPC TM-650 2.5.5.5, a design Dk of $3.00$ for 8 to 40 GHz by differential phase length, and a dissipation factor of $0.0010$ at 10 GHz and 23 °C by IPC TM-650 2.5.5.5. The thermal coefficient of Dk is $-3$ ppm/°C from $-50$ °C to $150$ °C at 10 GHz, and the moisture absorption is 0.04 % (D48/50, IPC TM-650 2.6.2.1). The footnotes describe the values as averages over the population and the design Dk as an average over several lots and the most common thicknesses. The table gives no absolute Dk below 10 GHz. Chart 3 on page index 2 (printed "3") plots a normalized Dk against frequency from 1 to 50 GHz, measured with the microstrip differential phase length method on 50 Ω lines on laminates about 20 mil thick. The chart does not say at which frequency the curves are normalized; all of them cross $1.00$ between about 20 and 25 GHz. Read by eye to about $\pm 0.002$, the RO3003 curve is about $1.010$ at 1 GHz, $1.005$ at 2 GHz and $1.001$ at 10 GHz, so the chart puts the RO3003 microstrip Dk at 1 GHz about 1 % above its value at 10 GHz. The curve labelled "High Performance FR-4" on the same chart is about $1.041$ at 1 GHz and $1.011$ at 10 GHz. Nothing below 1 GHz is plotted.

### Rogers RT/duroid 5880

The RT/duroid 5870/5880 data sheet (Rogers Publication #92-101, revision code 1603 080822, © 2022, two pages) gives, in the table on page index 1 (printed "Page 2 of 2"), a process Dk for RT/duroid 5880 of $2.20$ at 1 MHz by IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.3 and $2.20 \pm 0.02$, marked as the specification, at 10 GHz by the method printed as "IPC-TM 2.5.5.5", both at condition C24/23/50 in the z direction, and a design Dk of $2.20$ for 8 to 40 GHz by the differential phase length method. The dissipation factor is $0.0004$ at 1 MHz and $0.0009$ at 10 GHz. The thermal coefficient of Dk is $-125$ ppm/°C from $-50$ °C to $150$ °C, and the moisture absorption is 0.02 % (0.062 in sample, D48/50, ASTM D570). Footnote [1] states that the specification values are measured by IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.5 at about 10 GHz and 23 °C on laminates with 1 oz electrodeposited copper; it warns that these quality-acceptance values may not suit design work, especially for microstrip, and recommends verifying prototype boards. The same table gives RT/duroid 5870 a Dk of $2.33$ in all three entries. The data sheet has no chart, but because it prints the same Dk at 1 MHz and at 10 GHz, it brackets the 0.1 to 1 GHz band with a single value.

### Panasonic MEGTRON 6 (laminate R-5775, prepreg R-5670)

Panasonic's MEGTRON 6 data sheet (No. 260630, dated Jun. 2026, eleven slide-format pages) covers laminate R-5775 and prepreg R-5670 in the K and G versions; the cover (page index 0) explains that the letters in parentheses are internal identification codes. The general specification on page index 1 (printed "1") gives, for a 29.5 mil (0.750 mm, Core Type 30) sample at condition C-24/23/50, a Dk of $3.71$ at 1 GHz by IPC TM-650 2.5.5.9 and $3.62$ at 13 GHz by the balanced-type circular disk resonance method of IEC 63185 (2020), with dissipation factors of $0.002$ and $0.0046$, and a water absorption of 0.14 % (IPC TM-650 2.6.2.1). The following pages tabulate typical Dk by construction at 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 13, 24, 35, 46 and 56 GHz, with the 1 GHz column measured by IPC TM650-2.5.5.9, the 2 to 10 GHz columns by IPC TM650-2.5.5.5 and the 13 to 56 GHz columns by IEC 63185 (2020): laminate R-5775(K) on page index 2, prepreg R-5670(K) on index 4, laminate R-5775(G) on index 6 and prepreg R-5670(G) on index 8 (printed "2", "4", "6" and "8"), each followed by the matching Df table on the next page. Within each table the Dk depends only on the resin content. At 1 GHz the laminate values are $3.71$ at 54 % resin, $3.65$ at 57 %, $3.49$ at 63 %, $3.47$ at 64 % (G only), $3.46$ at 65 % and $3.41$ at 68 %, and the G table adds 7628-glass cores at 42 % resin with $4.07$; the prepreg values run from $3.71$ at 54 % resin to $3.28$ at 75 % in the K table and to $3.25$ at 77 % in the G table. The 10 GHz values are $0.08$ to $0.12$ lower than the 1 GHz values, for example $3.61$ for 54 % resin and $3.32$ for 68 %. In every row the step from 1 GHz to 2 GHz ($0.06$ to $0.10$) is larger than the whole change from 2 GHz to 10 GHz ($0.02$ to $0.05$); the two columns use different methods, and the tables do not separate the change of method from the frequency dependence. Df is $0.002$ at 1 GHz and $0.004$ at 10 GHz for every construction. Every table carries a note that the values are typical reference values and not guaranteed. The data sheet gives nothing below 1 GHz and has no chart.

### DuPont Pyralux AP

The Pyralux AP data sheet (DuPont document EI-10124 (2/25), © 2025 DuPont de Nemours, two pages without printed page numbers) is the file listed as the Pyralux AP technical data sheet on the Qnity Electronics product page. Table 3 on page index 1 gives typical values for AP9121, which Table 2 on page index 0 lists as 35 µm copper on a 50 µm (2 mil) all-polyimide dielectric: a Dk of $3.4$ at 1 MHz by IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.3 and $3.2$ at 10 GHz by ASTM D2520, with loss tangents of $0.002$ and $0.003$ at the same frequencies. A note under the table says that the values are typical for the listed product and that performance can vary with construction and processing. The moisture absorption is 0.8 % (IPC-TM-650 2.6.2), several times that of the rigid laminates in this file (0.02 % to 0.14 %). The document has no chart and no Dk between 1 MHz and 10 GHz, so it gives no value inside the 0.1 to 1 GHz band, only the values at the two ends of a wider range.

### Isola I-Tera MT40

The I-Tera MT40 data sheet (Isola, "Very Low-Loss Laminate and Prepreg", revision F, last updated 8 December 2025, three pages without printed page numbers) gives in its Typical Values Table on page index 1 a single Dk of $3.45$ for 2, 5 and 10 GHz by IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.5 and a single loss tangent of $0.0031$ for the same frequencies by the Bereskin stripline method. No tolerance, resin content or construction is attached to these values, and the moisture absorption is 0.1 % (IPC-TM-650 2.6.2.1A). The front page (index 0) repeats Dk $3.45$ and Df $0.0031$ in its header and says that the Dk is stable from $-55$ °C to $+125$ °C up to W-band frequencies, and page index 2 lists the revision history, in which revision F (3/24) added Advanced RTF copper and a 24 mil thickness. The data sheet gives nothing below 2 GHz and has no chart.

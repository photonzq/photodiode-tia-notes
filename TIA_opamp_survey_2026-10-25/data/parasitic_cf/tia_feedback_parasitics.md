# Parasitic feedback capacitance of TIA boards: what the board-level sources say

This file collects published values for the total capacitance across the feedback resistor of a transimpedance amplifier (TIA) on a printed-circuit board, and published layout measures with their measured effect. The quantity of interest is the total parasitic feedback capacitance $C_F$: the capacitance in parallel with the feedback resistor $R_F$ formed by the resistor body, its pads, the traces and vias between the amplifier output (or FB pin) and the inverting input, and the package pins. The resistor body alone is covered by a separate collection, so body-only values appear here only where a source compares them with a board value. The machine-readable version is `tia_feedback_parasitics.json` (39 records, one per source or per measured case). The arithmetic behind every inferred number is in `../../scripts_archive/parasitic_cf/inferred_feedback_c.py`, which writes `inferred_values.json` in this folder. The PDFs downloaded for this collection are in `../../appnotes/parasitic_cf/layout/`.

Every value marked "read" below was read on a rendered PDF page (or, for web articles, in the page text shown in a browser). Values marked "inferred here" are arithmetic on numbers read in the source. Sources that could not be read are listed at the end.

## How the inferred values were obtained

Most board-level numbers in the literature are inferred from a measured bandwidth or rise time, not measured with a capacitance bridge. When the bandwidth of a high-value TIA is limited by $R_F$ and its own shunt capacitance, the closed-loop transimpedance has a single pole at $f_{-3\,\mathrm{dB}} = 1/(2\pi R_F C_F)$, so

$$C_F \le \frac{1}{2\pi R_F f_{-3\,\mathrm{dB}}}, \qquad f_{-3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \frac{0.35}{t_r}, \tag{1}$$

where $t_r$ is the 10–90 % rise time of a single-pole response. The inequality holds because any other pole (amplifier, photodiode, light source, oscilloscope) also lowers the bandwidth. For example, the LTC6268-10 board with an 0805 resistor of $R_F = 402\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ had $t_r = 87$–$88\ \mathrm{ns}$, so $f_{-3\,\mathrm{dB}} = 0.35/87.5\ \mathrm{ns} = 4.0\ \mathrm{MHz}$ and $C_F = 1/(2\pi \times 402\ \mathrm{k\Omega} \times 4.0\ \mathrm{MHz}) = 99\ \mathrm{fF}$.

For the balanced detector of Masalov et al. (2017), the fitted shot-noise spectra give two parameters, $f^*$ and $p$, related to the circuit by the paper's Eq. (3),

$$p = \left(2\pi R_F C_F + \frac{1}{A_0 f_0}\right) f^*, \qquad\text{so}\qquad C_F = \frac{p/f^* - 1/(A_0 f_0)}{2\pi R_F}, \tag{2}$$

where $A_0 f_0$ is the gain-bandwidth product of the op amp (3.9 GHz for the OPA847, its data sheet p. 1). For the bare $12\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistor (no capacitor), Table I gives $f^* = 47.3 \pm 3\ \mathrm{MHz}$ and $p = 0.63 \pm 0.05$, so $p/f^* = 13.32\ \mathrm{ns}$, $1/(A_0 f_0) = 0.26\ \mathrm{ns}$ and $C_F = 13.06\ \mathrm{ns}/(2\pi \times 12\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = 0.173 \pm 0.018\ \mathrm{pF}$.

For the TI reference design TIDA-060025, whose edges are only a few times slower than the amplifier, a second-order model is used. With a single-pole op amp of gain-bandwidth $\omega_A = 2\pi\,\mathrm{GBW}$, a total input capacitance $C_T$ (photodiode, op-amp input and board, excluding $C_F$), and $R_F \parallel C_F$ in the feedback path,

$$\omega_n^2 = \frac{\omega_A}{R_F\,(C_F + C_T)}, \qquad \zeta = \frac{\omega_n}{2}\left(R_F C_F + \frac{1}{\omega_A}\right), \tag{3}$$

where $\omega_n$ is the natural frequency and $\zeta$ the damping of the closed-loop transimpedance. The 10–90 % edge of the step response is computed exactly from $\omega_n$ and $\zeta$. The model is calibrated against TI's own simulation (same circuit, $C_F = 75\ \mathrm{fF}$) and then solved for the $C_F$ that reproduces each measured edge.

## The sources, one paragraph each

### Board-level totals

**Linear Technology LTC6268-10 data sheet (626810f), pp. 14–15, and LT Journal of Analog Innovation, Vol. 25 No. 2 (July 2015), pp. 5–6 of the journal (PDF pp. 3–4).** A 402-kΩ TIA with an OSI FCI-125G-006 photodiode was built twice. With an 0805 resistor "in a basic circuit layout" the measured rise time was 87 ns in the data sheet (88 ns in the journal), which the author converts to 4 MHz and $C_F \approx 0.1\ \mathrm{pF}$. With a ground trace "between the resistor pads", in this case "much wider than that used in the case of the LTC6268, extending under the entire resistor dielectric", the total system rise time fell to 10.3 ns (34 MHz), and the author computes an upper limit of $C_F = 11.6\ \mathrm{fF}$, noting that attributing all of the bandwidth limit to $C_F$ "isn't fair". The journal's Figure 5(c) is a photograph of the board: the heavily shielded resistor is at position R9 on the bottom side with its feedback trace "entirely shielded", and a "much lighter dose" of shielding is shown at R12. The reduction is a factor of at least 8.5. The caption of the data-sheet Figure 6 gives the permittivity of FR4 and ceramic as about 4 (the journal says 5), and stresses that most of the capacitance is in the solids, not in the air.

**Analog Devices technical article "Transimpedance Amplifier Noise Considerations" (18 February 2015, LTC6268).** With an 0603 resistor of 499 kΩ "in a basic circuit layout", the measured bandwidth was 2.5 MHz, which the article converts to a total feedback capacitance of 0.13 pF. Two measures, "minimal pad sizing" and a short ground trace between the pads "near the TIA output end", raised the bandwidth to 11.2 MHz, a factor of more than 4. The article does not state the improved capacitance; by Eq. (1) it is at most $1/(2\pi \times 499\ \mathrm{k\Omega} \times 11.2\ \mathrm{MHz}) = 28.5\ \mathrm{fF}$. The trace near the output end "increases the output load capacitance very slightly". The article notes that a longer resistor separates the end caps but an 0805 has larger end caps than an 0603, so length alone does not help much. The same example is in the LTC6268 data sheet, pp. 14–15 (according to the Linear Technology article S54 by Black and Brisebois, PDF p. 3), which could not be downloaded.

**Linear Technology Demo Manual DC2414A (dc2414af; schematic Rev 3, 9 June 2016), pp. 1–3 and 5.** One board carries three SOT-23 TIA channels laid out for different parasitic feedback capacitances, on Nelco N4000-EP-SI laminate chosen for its relative permittivity of 3.4. The high-speed channel U3 (0603 resistor, top side, no vias, close to the op amp, little ground copper near the photodiode) has a parasitic $C_F$ of "about 0.1pF" and an 0402 footprint for an added capacitor. The "low C" channel U2 has an 0603 resistor on the bottom side with grounded copper placed "rather modestly" underneath: about 33 fF. The "very low C" channel U1 has a 1206 resistor on the bottom side with "a rather extreme layout": about 7 fF, which the manual calls "a simplification" derived from 100-ns rise times at 10 MΩ; the shield loads the resistive element, and Figure 2 models it as four sections of 2.5 MΩ ∥ 0.08 pF with 0.04 pF from each junction to ground. The manual states that "a typical 0805 resistor has about 70fF of capacitance in air" and that on FR-4 "the parasitic capacitances will increase by 30% to 40%". On FR-4 the three channels would therefore be about 0.13–0.14 pF, 43–46 fF and 9–10 fF (inferred here). It also reports that the high-speed channel oscillated at 1.4 GHz with a 12-pF photodiode and the "nominal 20k || 0.1pF" network until a grounded copper tape was placed along the bottom-side copper void.

**Texas Instruments TIDA-060025 (TIDUEL3A, November 2018, revised April 2022) with its BOM (TIDRXT2), schematic (TIDRXT3) and layer plots (TIDRXT6).** The OPA858 (WSON-8 with FB pin) drives a 10-kΩ TIA for a 1.1-pF photodiode. TI computes a required feedback capacitance of about 100 fF for $Q = 0.707$ (205 MHz) and 75 fF for $Q = 0.95$ (260 MHz), and builds the second as two 4.99-kΩ 0201 thick-film resistors in series (Yageo RC0201FR-074K99L), each in parallel with a 0.15-pF 0201 thin-film capacitor (AVX 02011JR15ZBSTR), which is TI's own rule of two series resistors when less than 0.15 pF is needed. A 10-Ω resistor sits between the network and IN−. The schematic note reads "Place supply decoupling caps and feedback network components as close to the pins as possible. Cut out planes under input and output pins." The six-layer board (ground on layers 2 and 5, 3.3 V and 5 V planes on layers 3 and 4) has the TIA on the bottom side, the bottom-layer pour cleared around it, and a drawn region at the TIA location on every inner plane, which in this kind of negative-plane print marks a copper void. Table 3-1 gives simulated edges of 1.20 ns (90–10 % fall) and 1.18 ns (10–90 % rise) and measured edges, for a 100-ns pulse, of 1.11 ns (fall) and 1.68 ns (rise); TI attributes the slow rise to the laser driver. With Eq. (3) calibrated to TI's simulation, the measured fall edge is reproduced with $C_F = 65\ \mathrm{fF}$, and with 51–72 fF over GBW = 4.5–6.5 GHz and $C_T$ = 1.7–2.1 pF (inferred here); the rise edges would need 115–117 fF. The amplifier-limited edge therefore shows no excess over the 75-fF nominal: the two 0201 resistors, their pads, the traces and the FB-to-IN− package coupling add too little to be seen, although the unknown tolerance of the 0.15-pF capacitors only allows a loose bound of a few tens of fF.

**Masalov, Kuzhamuratov and Lvovsky, "Noise spectra in balanced optical detectors based on transimpedance amplifiers", Rev. Sci. Instrum. 88, 113109 (2017), arXiv:1709.05213, pp. 2–3.** An OPA847 balanced detector with two Hamamatsu S5972 photodiodes, built after Kumar et al. "with an improved circuit layout to reduce the spurious capacitances and inductances", was measured with four feedback networks. Fits of the shot-noise spectra (within ±1 dB) used capacitor values of 0, 0.3, 0.3 and 0.4 pF "with a parasitic addition of 0.2, 0.2, 0.1 and 0 pF (respectively)" for $R_F$ = 12, 12, 4.3 and 2 kΩ. Solving the paper's Eq. (3) with its Table I gives a total of $0.173 \pm 0.018\ \mathrm{pF}$ for the bare 12-kΩ resistor, and parasitic additions of $0.26 \pm 0.05$, $0.07 \pm 0.03$ and $0.00 \pm 0.04\ \mathrm{pF}$ for the other three cases (inferred here). The resistor size and board are not given, the same fits require a total input capacitance of about 26 pF (far above the data-sheet values), and the 2-kΩ case tended to self-oscillate, so only the bare-resistor value is reasonably clean.

**Linear Technology Design Note 399, "Low Noise Amplifiers for Small and Large Area Photodiodes" (date code 09/06/399), p. 1.** A 1-MΩ TIA with an LTC6244HV and a 0.1-pF AVX Accu-F capacitor is said to have a total real feedback capacitance of "probably about 0.4pF", because the capacitor "is also in parallel with the op amp lead, resistor and parasitic capacitances". The measured 350-kHz bandwidth bounds the total by Eq. (1) at 0.45 pF (inferred here). This is the only source found that names the op-amp lead among the contributors, but it does not split the 0.3 pF of parasitics.

### Stated typical values and allocations

**TI OPA657 data sheet (SBOS197F, December 2001, revised August 2015), pp. 21–22.** In the 200-kΩ design example the chosen total feedback capacitance of 0.55 pF "includes the physical 0.4-pF feedback capacitor in addition to the 0.1-pF parasitic capacitance from the feedback resistor and around 50-fF capacitance from the PCB traces", and "removing the ground and power planes from under the surface mount components helps to minimize this parasitic capacitance". This is the only manufacturer statement found that splits the parasitic into a resistor part and a trace part (0.15 pF together); the measured output-noise spectrum (Figure 35) is said to match the simulation "very closely".

**TI OPA847 (SBOS251E), OPA846 (SBOS250E), OPA843 (SBOS268C), OPA699 (SBOS261D), THS4271 (SLOS397F) data sheets and the TI blog SSZTBC4 (May 2016).** These repeat that "good axial metal film or surface-mount resistors have approximately 0.2pF in shunt with the resistor" (OPA847 p. 19), that "typical surface-mount resistors have a parasitic capacitance of 0.2pF" (OPA847 p. 11), and that the shunt capacitance "may be as large as 0.2pF" (SSZTBC4 p. 1). No resistor size, layout or measurement is given. OPA847 p. 19 adds two layout rules: "always position the feedback and series output resistor, if any, as close as possible to the output pin", and "where double-side component mounting is allowed, place the feedback resistor directly under the package on the other side of the board between the output and inverting input pins".

**sci.electronics.design threads (ElectronDepot archive).** In "RF Resistors" (25–27 July 2022), Phil Hobbs writes that "the shunt capacitance of a SMT resistor all by itself is about 0.1 pF, and it's more like 0.05 pF when mounted on a ground-plane PC board", and that "the shunt capacitance across a resistor depends on the configuration of nearby conductors and dielectrics"; John Larkin puts an 0603 at "around 0.05 pF". In "Separating high pass and low pass TIA output bands" (2017), Hobbs gives "about 0.05 to 0.1 pF in parallel, depending on the ground configuration" and, for a four-layer board with ground on layer 2, "0.15 to 0.25 pF per pad" to ground, and says he puts "a bootstrapped pour under the summing node". These are informal, but they come from experienced designers and agree with the DC2414A numbers.

**The Art of Electronics: The x-Chapters, §4x.3.3 (publisher's sample, PDF p. 5, book p. 287).** Horowitz and Hill report that "by actual measurement" a standard RN55D-type metal-film resistor has "something like 0.07 pF – 0.15 pF of parasitic parallel capacitance", and describe a pole-zero network (Figure 4x.23) that cancels the resistor's own capacitance with an adjustable range of 0–0.2 pF. These are leaded parts, not surface-mount parts on a board.

**Burr-Brown AB-075, "Photodiode Monitoring with Op Amps" (1994/1995), pp. 3, 4 and 6.** The bulletin uses $C_S = 0.5\ \mathrm{pF}$ as the stray capacitance across a 100-MΩ resistor (3.2 kHz), mounts the resistor on Teflon standoffs, and discusses resistor and capacitor tee networks; it notes that a resistor tee reduces the stray across each element but that "stray capacitance from the op amp output to its input has the same effect as before".

**Other generic statements.** The LTC6228 data sheet (Rev. B, p. 20) illustrates the effect of "a parasitic capacitor of 400fF" across 5 kΩ (79 MHz). A blog by Bonnie Baker (Embedded Computing Design, 20 January 2020) says the cumulative parasitic across $R_F$ is "at the most a few picofarads" and "a careful layout can reduce this capacitance to below 1pF". An Opto Diode note (Jezdan Gomez, 2026, p. 7) calls it "commonly a few tenths of a picofarad and ... not controlled". These are too loose to use as numbers.

### Layout techniques

The quantified techniques are the shielding ground trace (factors of 4.5 to more than 8 in the LTC6268 examples; factors of 3 and about 14 between the DC2414A channels), small pads, a low-permittivity laminate (FR-4 is 30–40 % worse than $\varepsilon_r = 3.4$) and two resistors in series (TIDA-060025 shows no visible excess over the intended 75 fF). The shield works by turning end-to-end capacitance into capacitance from each end to ground: it adds a little load on the output (LTC6268 article) and, if it reaches the summing-node end, capacitance from the summing node to ground, which raises $C_T$ and therefore the noise gain of a low-$R_F$ TIA; the DC2414A manual models the result as a distributed RC line. Removing the ground and power planes under the feedback trace and the I/O pins (TI SSZTBC4 p. 4; OPA855 p. 29; OPA657 p. 22; ADI Analog Dialogue 39-09 and RAQ 34) mainly reduces the capacitance from those nodes to ground, which can reach "2 to 3 pF" at a carelessly laid-out summing node (RAQ 34, June 2008); it does not by itself reduce the end-to-end coupling. Keeping $R_F$ on the amplifier's side of the board avoids vias, each of which can add about 0.5 pF to ground on a 1.57-mm board (Analog Dialogue 39-09, September 2005); the LTC6268-10 photomultiplier board and the DC2414A high-speed channel both do this. Two or more resistors in series are also recommended by Hamamatsu (KSPD1043E05, p. 2) and by TI below 0.15 pF. Kumar et al. (Opt. Commun. 285, 5259 (2012), arXiv p. 7) trimmed the total with a twisted-wire capacitor across $R_F$, which shows that such boards are usually trimmed on the bench rather than predicted.

### Package and pins

No source gives a number for the capacitance between an op amp's output or FB pin and its inverting input. The OPA855 and OPA858 (WSON-8: FB pin 1, NC pin 2, IN− pin 3) put an unconnected pin between FB and IN−, and the OPA855 data sheet says this "minimizes the capacitive coupling between the FB and IN– pins by increasing the physical separation between the pins" (p. 17); the OPA858 data sheet says the NC pin "increases the physical spacing between them, thereby reducing parasitic coupling at high frequencies" (p. 16). The OPA818 (WSON-8: PD 1, FB 2, IN− 3, IN+ 4, VS− 5, NC 6, OUT 7, VS+ 8) has FB directly next to IN−, which allows "a very short feedback resistor (RF) connection" (p. 15); its SOT-23 version has no FB pin and puts OUT and IN− on opposite sides. ADI's dedicated feedback pins (AD8045, AD8099, ADA4899-1) are described the same way, and the LTC6228 data sheet asks for feedback from the FB pin of the SOIC-8 "to reduce signal trace length". TI's macromodels of OPA855, OPA858, OPA818 and OPA846 have no FB pin and no output-to-input coupling capacitor, so any package contribution has to be added to $C_F$ by the designer. DN399's "op amp lead" remark is the only hint that this term is not negligible.

### Context: capacitance to ground, not across $R_F$

Serikawa and Furusawa (arXiv:1803.06462v2, p. 5) fitted an excess of 0.9 pF at the photodiode node of a 500-MHz resonant detector on two-layer 1.6-mm FR-4 and attribute it to the PCB pattern. Bowden, Vianello and Hobson (arXiv:1910.11941v1, p. 2) removed "the ground planes under the signal path to ensure that the input capacitance is dominated by the diodes contribution". These values belong to the input-capacitance budget.

## What this means for a 2-kΩ TIA with an 0402 or 0201 resistor

At $R_F = 2\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ the corner $f = 1/(2\pi R_F C_F)$ is 796 MHz for 0.10 pF, 398 MHz for 0.20 pF and 265 MHz for 0.30 pF. The measured board totals for unshielded single resistors are 0.10 pF (0805, basic layout), 0.13 pF (0603, basic layout) and about 0.1 pF (0603 on the DC2414A high-speed channel, top side, low-permittivity laminate, which becomes 0.13–0.14 pF on FR-4); the bare 12-kΩ resistor of the Masalov detector fits to 0.17 pF, and TI's allocation for the OPA657 example is 0.15 pF (0.1 pF resistor plus 50 fF of traces). No source measures a single 0402 or 0201 on a board. Because the board, trace and package terms remain when the body shrinks, a careful single 0402 or 0201 layout next to an FB pin on FR-4 is most likely about 0.08–0.15 pF; this is an inference from the 0603/0805 data and the TIDA-060025 result, not a measurement. Unconsidered layouts sit at 0.15–0.3 pF (TI's "typical" 0.2 pF, the Masalov fit, DN399's estimate of about 0.3 pF of parasitics around a 0.1-pF capacitor). A shield trace reduces the total by factors of 3 to more than 8 (to 7–33 fF on the DC2414A and to at most 11.6–28.5 fF in the LTC6268 examples), at the cost of capacitance to ground at both ends. Table 1.1 of the note takes 0.08 pF, with corners at 0.05 and 0.12 pF, from the analytical estimate of its Appendix G, which covers the resistor, its pads and the amplifier's pins but not the traces; a layout with a shield could fall well below that.

## Input type

bipolar (classical differential input stage, PDF p1; 'very high-speed complementary bipolar process', PDF p19)

## Compensation

decompensated voltage feedback: optimized for a flat (Butterworth) response at a noninverting gain of +10, stable down to a noise gain of +7 (PDF p1, p16). External compensation techniques are given for lower gains (PDF pp. 12-14).

## Minimum stable gain

- **value**: 7
- **unit**: V/V (noise gain)
- **conditions**: noninverting; inverting with matched 50 ohm source (noise gain 1 + RF/(2RG)) the minimum stable inverting gain is -12 V/V
- **datasheet_statements**:
  - Features: 'STABLE FOR GAINS >= 7' (PDF p1).
  - 'optimized for a flat response at a gain of +10 and is stable down to a gain of +7' (PDF p1).
  - 'When operating the amplifier at a noise gain less than +7, increased peaking and possible sustained oscillations may result. However, operation at low gains may be desirable to take advantage of the higher slew rate and exceptional DC precision' (PDF pp. 13-14).
  - Inverting with source matched by RG: noise gain 1 + RF/(2RG) 'increasing the minimum stable gain for inverting operation under these conditions to -12V/V and increases the equivalent GBP to > 3.5GHz' (PDF p16).
  - 'Unity-gain stability in the op amp is not required for application as a transimpedance amplifier' (PDF p11).
- **below_minimum_gain_methods**:
  - Inverting low-gain compensation (PDF p12, Figure 5): capacitor CS from the inverting node to ground and CF across RF set the high-frequency noise gain NG2 = 1 + CS/CF above the minimum stable gain, while NG1 = 1 + RF/RG sets the low-frequency gain; Eq. 4 ZO = GBP/NG1^2 [(1 - NG1/NG2) - sqrt(1 - 2 NG1/NG2)], Eq. 5 CF = 1/(2 pi RF ZO NG2), Eq. 6 CS = (NG2 - 1) CF, Eq. 7 f-3dB ~ sqrt(ZO GBP). Example NG1 = 3 (gain -2), NG2 = 10.5, RG 250, RF 500, CF 2.9 pF, CS 27 pF: 142 MHz predicted; text says 170 MHz measured with flatness through 70 MHz (Figure 6; CURVE: 6.12 dB flat, +0.05 dB near 70 MHz, -3 dB at about 175 MHz). Keeps full slew rate and noise performance and improves distortion (Figure 7: HD2 within about 2 dB of the G = +10 case, HD3 about 1.5-2 dB lower at 10-15 MHz). Low Gain Inverting Bandwidth plot (PDF p6, RF = 400, external compensation per Figure 5): -3 dB G = -1 138 MHz, G = -2 161 MHz, G = -4 and G = -6 217 MHz (G = -6 peaks +0.4 dB near 100 MHz) (CURVE).
  - Noninverting low-gain compensation (PDF p14, Figure 9): resistor R1 across the two inputs so that 1 + RF/(RG || R1) > 7; retains slew rate 'but gives up some of the low-noise benefit'. Example gain +2: RF = RG = 402, R1 = 65, RT = 50; Figure 10 (CURVE): 6.0 dB flat to 100 MHz, +0.27 dB at 300 MHz, -3 dB at about 560 MHz. Gain-flatness tuning plot (PDF p6) for Av = +8 (RF 453, RG 64.9) with NG 8.0-10.0: NG 9.5 stays within +0.08/-0.1 dB to about 216 MHz; NG 10 is -0.1 dB at 123 MHz; NG 8.0-9.0 peak above +0.1 dB from about 50-67 MHz (CURVE).

## Application guidance

- Figure 1 (G = +10) and Figure 2 (G = -20) are the specification test circuits; effective load 83 ohm in Figure 1 (100 ohm || 503 ohm feedback network). RG = 50 ohm recommended starting point to keep resistor noise below the 1.2 nV/rtHz amplifier noise (PDF pp. 10, 18).
- Inverting operation: driving source must have very low output impedance beyond the closed-loop bandwidth for stability (PDF p10).
- Noise model Figure 13 and Eqs. 8-10: a 90 ohm source on +IN adds Johnson noise equal to the amplifier en; with RS = 50 ohm and NG > 10 the total input noise is 1.7 nV/rtHz (PDF p15).
- Low-noise-figure IF amplifier, Figure 8: 1:2 transformer, RG 200, RF 1 kohm || 2 pF, CS 20 pF, +IN grounded; NG 3.5 (below minimum stable gain, handled by inverting low-gain compensation); NF 6 dB, 14 dB gain, 140 MHz bandwidth, +/-0.1 dB to 50 MHz; 72 dBc SFDR for two 4 dBm tones at 20 MHz (PDF p13).
- Differential (two amplifiers, inverting): about 5 dB better HD2 and similar HD3 at 10 MHz versus single channel (PDF p14).
- Layout: open ground/power planes under the signal I/O pins; 0.1 uF within 0.25 inch of supply pins plus 2.2-6.8 uF bulk; SMD resistors; feedback resistor close to the output pin or under the package on the other side; no sockets; matched or series-terminated lines for longer traces (PDF pp. 18-19).
- SPICE model available; it includes the specified noise terms and does not distinguish packages (PDF p15). Demo boards DEM-OPA-SO-1B (SBOU026) and DEM-OPA-SOT-1B (SBOU027) (PDF p15).

## Absolute maximum ratings and input protection

- **supply**: +/-6.5 VDC
- **differential_input**: +/-1.2 V
- **input_voltage_range**: +/-VS
- **junction_temperature**: +150 C
- **storage**: -65 to +125 C
- **lead_temperature**: +300 C (soldering, 10 s)
- **ESD**: HBM 2000 V, CDM 1500 V, MM 200 V
- **internal_protection**: ESD diodes from every pin to both supplies (Figure 17); moderate protection against input overdrive above the supplies; diodes typically support 30 mA continuous; add series current-limiting resistors on both inputs if higher currents are possible, keeping them small (noise and bandwidth) (PDF p19).
- **thermal**: TJ = TA + PD x thetaJA; worst case output-stage dissipation VS^2/(4 RL) with RL including the feedback network; example OPA846IDBV in Figure 1 at 85 C driving 100 ohm at +2.5 VDC: PD = 10 V x 13.9 mA + 5^2/(4 x (100 || 500)) = 214 mW, TJ = 117 C (PDF p18).

## Other notes, inconsistencies and uncertainties

- Table row 'Peaking at a Gain of +7' has no value (PDF p3).
- Typical open-loop plot shows 93.7 dB at low frequency versus the 90 dB table typical.
- The p8 output-current-vs-temperature plot (128 mA sourcing, 86 mA sinking at 25 C) does not match the table typical of +/-80 mA at VO = 0; the plot conditions are not stated.
- Eq. 5 (PDF p12) prints CF = 2.86 pF but with RF = 500 ohm, ZO = 11.6 MHz, NG2 = 10.5 the formula gives 2.62 pF; Eq. 6 then gives the printed CS = 24.9 pF (9.5 x 2.62), while Figure 5 uses CF = 2.9 pF and CS = 27 pF.
- Figure 7 (PDF p13) y-axis labelled 'Gain (dB)' with tick labels -65, -70, -75, -85, -85, -90, -95 (duplicated -85).
- Differential frequency-response plots (PDF p9) label the x axis 'Frequency (Hz)' with 1 to 1k; values are MHz.
- Harmonic Distortion vs Noninverting Gain (PDF p5) x axis printed 'Gain (-V/V)'.
- Thermal example uses 13.9 mA quiescent current, above the 13.2 mA over-temperature maximum in the table.
- Ordering table (PDF p2) lists 'Rails, 100' for OPA846ID; the 2025 addendum lists a tube of 75.
- The datasheet gives one current-noise figure without specifying the input; for a TIA the inverting-input current noise is the relevant one.

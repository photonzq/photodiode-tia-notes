# Scripts for the parasitic feedback capacitance

These scripts support Appendix G of the note, the analytical estimate of the capacitance across the feedback resistor, and the collections of measured values, dimensions and permittivities in `../../data/parasitic_cf/`. Those in `numerical/` also support the side note `TIA_feedback_layouts` at the root of the repository, on eight-lead packages, guard rings and the inductance of the leads. Each script takes its paths as arguments or works relative to its own folder. Run the scripts that read downloaded PDFs in isolated mode (`python -I`).

## The estimate and its figures

| Script | What it does | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| `cf_parasitic_estimate.py` | Evaluates every formula of Appendix G (Eqs. (G.14)–(G.47)) for 0201–0805 resistors, the OPA855 pins, a ground plane and a guard strip, in both mounting orientations. It also runs eleven numerical confirmations (C1–C11) of the special functions, the maps and the limiting cases. | `python cf_parasitic_estimate.py [--json <out.json>]` |
| `make_parasitic_cf_figures.py` | Draws Figures 39–44 of Appendix G (`figures/fig38_parasitic_cf_*` to `fig43_parasitic_cf_*`, PNG at 600 dpi and PDF). The potentials in Figures 41–43 come from the closed-form maps, not from a mesh. | `python make_parasitic_cf_figures.py [outdir]` (default `../../figures`) |
| `inductance_estimates.py` | Estimates what the electrostatic model leaves out: the resistor's own series inductance (as $-L_s/R^2$ in parallel), the partial self-inductance of the routing and vias (Neumann formula), its effect on $R_F \parallel C_F$ at each design's loop crossover, the inductance in series with the photodiode, and (part F, Section 4 of the side note `TIA_feedback_layouts`) the leads of the SOT-23-5, SOIC-8 and PDIP-8 packages, with the loop crossover below which the inductance changes $C_F$ by less than 1 %. Every formula is derived in its comments; it prints every input and substitution. | `python inductance_estimates.py` (reads `../../data/parasitic_cf/numerical/resistor_3d.json`) |
| `fasthenry_validation.py` | Checks `inductance_estimates.py` with FastHenry (FastHenry2, through its automation interface): the 0.2-mm strips and the formula (B3), the via and PDIP-8 hole as tubes (cages of 8–64 strips) and as the field models' square barrels, one lead of each package, the whole series path of the SOT-23-5, SOIC-8 and PDIP-8 layouts (leads, vias and traces, mutual terms included), and the SOT-23-5 and SOIC-8 paths over the field models' windowed ground planes. Each high-frequency result at three filament levels and each plane result at three grid sizes; inputs, outputs and the convergence tables in `../../data/parasitic_cf/fasthenry/`. `report` also computes, from the records alone, every number that Appendix C and Section 4 of the side note quote (Eq. (C.1) for the PDIP-8 lead, $L_x$ and $\delta_1$ with the FastHenry paths, the convergence ranges). | `python fasthenry_validation.py run <group>\|all`, then `report` (needs FastHenry2 and pywin32; `report` needs neither) |

## Reading the sources

| Script | What it does | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| `find_parasitic_statements.py` | Locates the pages of local PDFs that state a parasitic or feedback capacitance, so that those pages can be rendered and read. | `python find_parasitic_statements.py <pdf or folder> [...]` |

| `pdf_pages.py` | Finds pages matching a pattern, and renders chosen pages to PNG for reading. | `python -I pdf_pages.py find <pdf> <regex>` and `python -I pdf_pages.py render <pdf> <out_dir> --pages 1-3` |
| `dump_pdf_vector_paths.py` | Dumps the vector paths and text spans of one PDF page, so that plotted curves drawn as vector graphics can be read numerically. | `python -I dump_pdf_vector_paths.py <pdf> <page> <out.json> [--clip x0,y0,x1,y1]` |
| `read_vector_impedance_curves.py` | Converts vector-drawn $\lvert Z\rvert/R$ curves into the equivalent parallel capacitance at each point. The axis calibrations are in `curve_specs/`. | see the script's docstring |
| `trace_raster_curve.py` | Reads a curve off a bitmap plot of $\lvert Z\rvert/R$ and converts it in the same way. | `python -I trace_raster_curve.py axes <image.png>`, then the tracing mode in the docstring |
| `merge_curve_readings.py` | Merges the curve readings into `data/parasitic_cf/resistor_curve_readings.csv`. | `python merge_curve_readings.py <out.csv> "<label>=<file>" ...` |
| `inferred_feedback_c.py` | Turns the bandwidths, rise times and fits printed in the board-level sources into feedback capacitances. It writes `data/parasitic_cf/inferred_values.json`. | `python -I inferred_feedback_c.py [out.json]` |
| `build_geometry_permittivity_json.py` | Regenerates `data/parasitic_cf/geometry_and_permittivity.json`, the resistor dimensions, land patterns and permittivities read from the manufacturer documents (0201 to 0805, and the 1206 of the thick-film makers and of Vishay's land patterns, added for the eight-lead study). | `python -I build_geometry_permittivity_json.py <out.json>` |

## Editing the note

The estimate was first written as a standalone document with its own equation numbers (P1)–(P49), tables P1–P3, figures P1–P6 and reference list [P1]–[P8]. That draft is not kept; it became Appendix G. The first two scripts below were applied to it and match its text, not the appendix.

| Script | What it did | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| `fill_derivation_numbers.py` | Brought the worked examples of the draft in line with the sourced inputs, after the dimensions and permittivities were collected. | `python fill_derivation_numbers.py <draft.md>` |
| `apply_review_fixes.py` | Applied the corrections of an independent review of the draft: one false claim, small slips, the gaps it found (two-dimensional decay, Rule 6 with cuts and floating conductors, onto-ness of two maps, the Landen step, 1/ln h, the film over the pads, the film-pattern equation for a patterned film) and the notation clashes. | `python apply_review_fixes.py <draft.md>` |
| `floor_effect.py` | Evaluated the flat, 1-dB and robust designs of OPA855, OPA858 and OPA955 with the floor of the design search at 0.20 and at 0.08 pF. It showed that a floor at the parasitic would move the 2-kΩ robust designs of OPA855 and OPA858 to 0.14–0.15 pF, where Section 6.2.8 finds little margin left at the corners, which is why the designs keep a minimum total of 0.2 pF. | `python floor_effect.py <scripts_archive folder>` |
| `make_appendix_g.py` | Turned the draft into Appendix G: section, equation, table and figure numbers; the note's citation style and the references [A25]–[A34] and [B3]–[B5] of Appendix D; captions for the tables that had none; and the parts that state how the note uses the estimate. | `python make_appendix_g.py <draft.md> <appendix_G.md>` |
| `revise_note_cfpar.py` | Revised the note to $C_{F,\mathrm{par}}$ = 0.08 pF (corners 0.05–0.12 pF) with a minimum total $C_F$ of 0.2 pF, added the references and files of Appendix G to Appendices D and E, and appended Appendix G. It checks the note's hash before editing. | `python revise_note_cfpar.py <note.md> <appendix_G.md> <sha256 prefix>` |
| `patch_scripts_appendix_g.py` | Pointed `cf_parasitic_estimate.py` and `make_parasitic_cf_figures.py` at Appendix G and at the figure files `fig38`–`fig43`. | `python patch_scripts_appendix_g.py <this folder>` |
| `patch_analysis_labels.py` | Brought the comments and table labels of `run_analysis.py`, `case_studies.py`, `medium_gain.py` and `starting_values.py` in line with Table 1.1; only `starting_values.py` changes a number, the external capacitor of Table A8.13 (total minus 0.08 pF). | `python patch_analysis_labels.py <scripts_archive folder>` |
| `show_sentences.py`, `show_par_lines.py`, `list_refs.py` | Listed the sentences that use a value, and every cross-reference of a Markdown file, while the revision was mapped. | see each docstring |

## Field solutions (Section G.14), in `numerical/`

These scripts use the finite-element package `pcbcap` at the root of the repository (its `README.md` describes it, and each module derives what it does). They write their records and logs to `../../data/parasitic_cf/numerical/`. Each case runs in its own process at below-normal priority; `rerun_all.sh` recomputes everything in order.

| Script | What it does | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| `numerical/g_closed_forms_2d.py` | Solves the idealised two-dimensional problem behind each exact closed form of Sections G.6–G.11 and compares (Table G.10); also gives the values that the closed forms only bracketed (Section G.14.3). | `python g_closed_forms_2d.py list`, then `run <case> [--record <json>]` |
| `numerical/g_resistor_3d.py` | Three-dimensional chip resistors on their pads: convergence and enclosure studies for 0201–0805, the permittivity corners, the flipped part, the construction details, other board materials (with the enclosure correction for three of them), and the OPA855 network of Figure 44. Cases `fillet_0402_views` (drawings, no solve) and `fillet_0402` give the solder fillets of Table D.2 of the side note `TIA_feedback_layouts`, with the share of $C_F$ held in each medium. | `python g_resistor_3d.py list`, then `run <case> [--record <json>]` |
| `numerical/g_opa846_3d.py` | The OPA846 in SOT-23-5 with its feedback resistor in three layouts and two sizes, the contributions of the resistor, the package and the planes, and two checks of the numerical choices. | `python g_opa846_3d.py list`, `estimate`, then `run <case> [--record <json>]` |
| `numerical/g_eight_lead_3d.py` | Feedback networks around amplifiers in eight-lead packages (pcbcap.packages.Soic8 and Dip8): the OPA846 in SO-8 in three layouts (resistor under the package with vias in the pads or under the body, or on the top side beyond the package), and high-gain layouts with a 1206 or 0805: the OPA637 in SOIC-8, the ADA4530-1 and LMP7721 in SOIC-8 with the guard layouts of their data sheets, and the LMC6001 in PDIP-8 with and without a guard ring. Its docstring states the mesh policy and the memory limit. The side note `TIA_feedback_layouts` reports the results. | `python g_eight_lead_3d.py list`, `estimate`, then `run <case> [--record <json>]` |
| `numerical/g_guard_ladder.py` | The guarded layouts of `g_eight_lead_3d.py` (and the unguarded PDIP-8 one) with the film divided into 10 finite elements (pcbcap.distributed): the capacitance matrix of their hat functions, and from it the feedback impedance and the film's noise against frequency, compared with the lumped first-order model, each number with the error estimate of five against ten elements (Section 7 of the side note `TIA_feedback_layouts`). About seven minutes per case. | `python g_guard_ladder.py run <case> --record <json>`, then `response <json> [--figure <stem>] [--out <json>]` |
| `numerical/g_guard_loop.py` | The same networks inside the transimpedance loop of their amplifiers on the high-gain note's 20-pF photodiode, at 1, 10 and 100 GΩ, against the lumped first-order network and the network alone (Table 8 of the side note). No solve. | `python g_guard_loop.py [--out <json>]` |
| `numerical/make_side_note_figures.py` | Draws Figures 1–4 of the side note `TIA_feedback_layouts` from the records and the layout builders. No solve. | `python make_side_note_figures.py [numbers] [--out <folder>]` (default: the side note's `figures/`) |
| `numerical/run_eight_lead.sh` | Runs every case of `g_eight_lead_3d.py`, one process per case, recording into `eight_lead_3d.json` and logging to `eight_lead_3d.log`; the last two cases check the coarser mesh level of the PDIP cases and the mesh policy against the published SOT-23-5 result. About 20 minutes. | `bash run_eight_lead.sh [python]` |
| `numerical/render_layout_views.py` | Draws every three-dimensional layout of the study scripts from six sides (PNG at 600 dpi and PDF) and writes an interactive HTML page for each (pcbcap.webview, plotly), with an index page, into `figures/layout_views/`. No mesh is made. | `python render_layout_views.py [out_dir]` |
| `numerical/run_all_3d.sh`, `numerical/run_opa846.sh` | Run every case of the two scripts above, one process per case, logging each one. | `bash run_all_3d.sh [python]`, `bash run_opa846.sh [python]` |
| `numerical/rerun_all.sh` | Deletes and recomputes every record of Section G.14: the package's checks, the 2-D comparisons, both 3-D batches and the region split of the 0402, after copying the old records to a backup folder if one is given. About 1 h 45 min on a workstation that was also in other use. | `bash rerun_all.sh [python] [backup_folder]` |
| `numerical/inspect_layouts.py` | Builds the OPA855 and OPA846 layouts as the solver receives them, draws them from above, in perspective and in a section, checks that every net's metal is one connected piece, and lists every item with its extent. No mesh is made. | `python inspect_layouts.py [out_dir]` |
| `numerical/field_regions.py` | Splits $C_{F,\mathrm{par}}$ of the 0402 model (the mesh of Figures 46c and 47) by region (alumina between the wraps and inside the terminations, FR-4 under the gap and elsewhere, air under the body and elsewhere), by sign and by distance from the part, for the text beside Figure 47. | `python field_regions.py [--record <json>]` |
| `numerical/make_numerical_figures.py` | Draws Figures 45–52, in the files `fig44`–`fig51`, from the records (Figures 46c and 47 solve the 0402 model once). | `python make_numerical_figures.py [file numbers 44–51]` |
| `numerical/development/` | The probes and audit checks written while the package and these cases were built, kept as they were run; its `README.md` lists them. | see that README |
| `numerical/numerical_section_template.md`, `numerical/add_numerical_section.py` | The text of Section G.14 with placeholders, and the script that fills them from the records and makes the edits elsewhere in the note (G.1, G.8, G.11, G.13.5, Table G.6, Appendices C–E), checking the note's hash first. | `python add_numerical_section.py <note.md> <sha256 prefix> [--dry-run <out.md>]` |
| `numerical/check_template_mirror.py` | Checks that the note's Section G.14 still holds every line of the template in order, each placeholder standing for any text, and counts the lines of G.14 that the note alone holds. | `python check_template_mirror.py [note.md] [template.md]` |
| `numerical/fastercap_validation.py` | Checks Section G.14 with FasterCap through `pcbcap.fastercap`: a cube in air and in a dielectric (known answers), the 0402 model without its film in the same grounded 8-mm box as pcbcap (the like-for-like test) and in free space on boards of 16–64 mm, and the published 0402 with its film as 10, 20 and 40 strips; and the two tests of the side note's Appendix D, the box model in a uniform medium and with every face of its metal cut into panels no wider than the metal is thick, and both again with every panel near the part 30 µm or smaller. `export` writes the input, checks every kind of panel's area against gmsh's fragments and draws the panels from six sides (`data/parasitic_cf/fastercap/views/`); `view` writes a case into another folder, lists every box of the part and draws it at true proportions; `run` runs FasterCap at a series of tolerances; `pcbcap` runs the film-free 0402 in pcbcap, with its dielectrics or in a uniform medium; `fastcap2` converts a case to the format of FastCap 2.0, a second boundary-element program, runs it through FastCap2's automation object and keeps FastCap2's console output with the time each piece arrived, and records how far a stopped run got (set-up or the GMRES iteration of each column); `fastcap2-plan` predicts, from the panels alone, the partitioning depth FastCap2 will choose, including its widening of the root cube when one panel is wider than the spread of the panel centres, the memory of its cube arrays and the work of its preconditioner. The convergence tables go into `data/parasitic_cf/fastercap/README.md`. | `python fastercap_validation.py export all`, `view <case> --out <folder>`, `run <case> [--tol ...] [--galerkin]`, `pcbcap r0402_nofilm` or `r0402_nofilm_air`, `fastcap2 <case> [--options "-o3 -t0.001"]`, `fastcap2-plan <case> [--depth n]`, `report` |
| `numerical/fastcap2_large_panels.py` | Reads the FastCap 2.0 inputs of the fine 0402 cases and measures every panel larger than 2 mm (the box's faces and the board's coarse faces): the extent of the panel centres and FastCap2's widened root cube, and for each large panel the potential its uniformly spread charge produces at the metal's centroid (Gauss–Legendre quadrature at two orders, and the equal-area disc of Eq. (D.7) of the side note `TIA_feedback_layouts`) against that of the same charge at the panel's centroid, as FastCap2's expansions take it. No solve. | `python fastcap2_large_panels.py [case ...]` |

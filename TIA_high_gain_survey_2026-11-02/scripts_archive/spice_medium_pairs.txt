### 9.8 The designs at 10 kΩ, where the notes meet

Section 6.9 ranks the fast parts at 10 kΩ with this note's single-pole model, which leaves out the excess phase of their open-loop curves, and compares that model with the companion's, which includes it. This check runs the vendors' macromodels of those parts at 10 kΩ, so that the ranking and the comparison do not rest on the two models alone. It covers two settings.

- **The 20-pF photodiode of the sweep**, in the decks of Section 9.1. OPA818, OPA858, OPA855 and LMH6629 are each run at three designs: the sweep's own design, and the designs of Table 6.8 held to 10 MHz and to 30 MHz, the bandwidths at which that table ranks the FET parts first and the bipolar parts first. Table 9.7 gives the results.
- **The companion's node**, a 1-pF photodiode pair with 0.35 pF of board capacitance, with the companion's supplies and loads: ±2.5 V and 200 Ω for OPA855, and ±6.5 V and 100 Ω for OPA818. Each of the two parts is run at this note's design for 0.1 pF of feedback parasitic, 0.100 pF for OPA855 and 0.195 pF for OPA818, and at the companion's design for its nominal 0.2 pF, which is 0.206 pF for both (its Table A6.38). The loop gain is read by Middlebrook's double injection, which combines a voltage and a current injected at the amplifier's output into the loop gain of the intact circuit, as in the companion's Section 5.2. Table 9.8 compares SPICE with both notes' models at the same $C_F$.

The macromodels are TI's for OPA855 (SBOMB43D, Rev. D), OPA858 (SBOMBK7) and LMH6629 (SNOM133, with its COMP pin high, the mode for gains of 10 and more), the files that the companion uses, and OPA818's model of Section 9.1. The 16 designs run in a few seconds (`hg_spice_medium.py`).

*Table 9.7. The designs at 10 kΩ on the 20-pF photodiode (S1336-18BK) in SPICE against the analytic model: the sweep's own design and the designs of Table 6.8 held to 10 MHz and 30 MHz. $\Delta N$ is the excess noise of Eq. (16) over [1 Hz, $B$] for both, where $B$ is the model's bandwidth for the sweep's design and the column's bandwidth for the designs of Table 6.8. Both bandwidths are interpolated on the SPICE run's grid of 40 points per decade, which reads the model's 30 MHz as 29.7 MHz. `hg_spice_medium.py`.*

<!-- table: table_9_7 -->

**On the 20-pF photodiode the macromodels confirm the model and the ranking of Table 6.8.** The bandwidths agree within 3.1% in eleven of the twelve designs, and no design peaks by more than 0.11 dB. The exception is LMH6629 held to 30 MHz, which reaches 26.8 MHz in SPICE, 9.8% short; its sweep's design is 2.7% short. The excess noise agrees within 0.52 dB, and within 0.84 dB for LMH6629's own design, whose macromodel is 9% quieter at 30 MHz because it carries less input capacitance than its data sheet gives, as the companion found at 2 kΩ (its Section 5.6). Both crossings of Table 6.8 hold. At 10 MHz the FET parts come within 5.6–5.8 dB of the floor in SPICE and the bipolar parts within 7.2–8.0 dB, against 5.3–5.9 dB and 7.3–8.0 dB in the model. At 30 MHz the bipolar parts lead: LMH6629 and OPA855 come within 8.3 and 9.3 dB, and OPA818 and OPA858 within 13.4 and 13.5 dB, against 8.8, 9.5, 13.0 and 13.8 dB in the model. OPA818's noise density is 1–6% higher in SPICE, as at the higher gains of Section 9.2, and that of OPA858 and OPA855 is 1–4% lower.

*Table 9.8. The designs at 10 kΩ on the companion's node, a 1-pF photodiode pair with 0.35 pF of board capacitance, with the companion's supplies and loads: SPICE against the companion's open-loop model and this note's single-pole model, at the same $C_F$. The phase margin in SPICE comes from Middlebrook's double injection. The noise is the band average of the input-referred noise over 10 MHz to the lower of 150 MHz and the companion model's bandwidth, against the Johnson noise of $R_F$, the measure of the companion's Table A6.38. `hg_spice_medium.py`.*

<!-- table: table_9_8 -->

**On the companion's node the macromodels' bandwidths lie between the two models'.** For OPA855 SPICE gives 215 MHz at 0.100 pF, between the companion's 207 MHz and this note's 219 MHz, with 67.5° of phase margin against 69.4° and 68.9°. At 0.206 pF it gives 84 MHz and 78.8°, which the companion's model reproduces within 0.2% and 0.4°, while this note's gives 86 MHz and 84.4°. OPA855's noise is 0.3 dB below both models'. OPA818 shows the spread between the two models that Section 6.9 found. SPICE gives 120 MHz at 0.195 pF and 115 MHz at 0.206 pF, while the companion's model gives 12% more and this note's 7% less, and its phase margin of 55–57° lies 9° above the companion's and 9° below this note's. OPA818's noise is 2.2 dB above the companion's central estimate and 1.4 dB above this note's, because its macromodel's noise density at tens of megahertz is higher than either model's: 1.82 pA/√Hz at 10 MHz, against 1.40 and 1.52 pA/√Hz. The companion found the same at 2 kΩ, where OPA818's macromodel had 1.6 dB more noise and 15.5° more phase margin than its analysis (its Section 5.6). At 10 kΩ, then, the single-pole model comes as close to the macromodels as the companion's open-loop model does: within 2.3% in bandwidth for OPA855 and 7.2% for OPA818, against 3.6% and 12.3%. This supports 10 kΩ as the point where the two notes meet.
